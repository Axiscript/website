Ask Chief
We're opening a bellhole over a 6 inch steel main and it's 6 feet deep. Do I need a ladder in there, and how close does it have to be?
Checking the library
Writing answer

Yes, you need a ladder, stairway, ramp or other safe way out. It has to be placed so nobody has more than 25 feet of lateral travel to reach it.

- The rule applies to trench excavations 4 feet or more deep. At 6 feet, you're over that line. (29 CFR 1926.651 Specific excavation requirements.) - A bellhole is usually small enough that one ladder is within 25 feet of everybody in it. If it's a long open cut, you may need more than one. - At 6 feet deep you're also past 5 feet, so the hole needs a cave-in protective system (sloping, benching, shield or shoring) unless it's entirely in stable rock. (29 CFR 1926.652 Requirements for protective systems.) The project spec governs. Check it for any tighter ladder or egress requirement.
The bellhole is only about 8 feet long. Does the 25-foot rule still apply, and is one ladder enough?
Checking the library
Writing answer

Yes, the 25-foot rule still applies, but in an 8 foot long hole one ladder is enough. Nobody in there is more than 25 feet of lateral travel from it.

- The rule covers trench excavations 4 feet or more deep, and it requires a stairway, ladder, ramp or other safe means of egress so employees have no more than 25 feet of lateral travel. (29 CFR 1926.651 Specific excavation requirements.) - If the bellhole grows into a longer open cut, recheck the distance and add ladders as needed. - Still at 6 feet deep, so you need a protective system unless it's all in stable rock. Under 5 feet is the only other exception. (29 CFR 1926.652 Requirements for protective systems.) Not sure if OSHA calls a bellhole a "trench" or just an "excavation". Most crews put a ladder in anyway. Check with your safety lead if it matters. The project spec governs. Check the Willow Run Connector spec for any tighter egress requirement.

How do I calculate a 1.5:1 Class C excavation for this bellhole?
Checking the library
Thinking it over
Searching the web
Searching the web: OSHA 1926 Subpart P Appendix B Type C soil maximum allowable slope 1½...
Reading 10 sources
Writing answer

Multiply the bellhole depth by 1.5 to get the horizontal run for each side.

That's how far back the slope has to go. OSHA calls it Type C soil, not Class C. - Top width = bottom width + (2 × 1.5 × depth). Example: a 10 ft deep trench in Type C soil must be sloped back 15 ft on each side. (Toolgrit trench guide) - 1.5:1 is 34 degrees from horizontal. (OSHA 1926 Subpart P App B) - The 1.5:1 slope applies to simple slope excavations 20 feet or less in depth. If it's deeper than 20 ft, I'm not sure, so check with the engineer. (OSHA 1926 Subpart P App B) - The actual slope shall not be steeper than the maximum allowable slope. You can go flatter, not steeper. (OSHA 1926 Subpart P App B) - If you see signs of distress, cut the slope back at least 1/2 horizontal to one vertical less steep than the maximum allowable slope. For Type C, that means 2:1. (GovRegs App B) - Check that the competent person has classified the soil as Type C. The slope only works if the soil call is right. The project spec governs.
This bellhole is 5 feet deep. Bottom is 6 feet long and 4 feet wide. We've got the top at 15 by 13 right now. Is that within the rule? And do I figure the length the same way as the width?
Checking the library
Thinking it over
Writing answer

No, it's not within the rule.

At 5 ft deep, 1.5:1 needs a top of 21 ft by 19 ft, and you're at 15 by 13. Yes, you figure the length the same way as the width. Each side that's sloped gets 1.5 × depth, which is 7.5 ft per side. - Length: 6 + (2 × 7.5) = 21 ft. - Width: 4 + (2 × 7.5) = 19 ft. - Your 15 by 13 top works out to 4.5 ft of run per side over 5 ft of depth. That's 0.9:1, which is steeper than 1.5:1. - The actual slope can't be steeper than the maximum allowable slope. (29 CFR Appendix B to Subpart P of Part 1926 Appendix B to Subpart P of Part 1926 - Sloping and Benching) - If there are signs of distress, or equipment, spoil, or traffic is close, the competent person has to flatten it more than 1.5:1. (29 CFR Appendix B to Subpart P of Part 1926 Appendix B to Subpart P of Part 1926 - Sloping and Benching) - This all assumes the competent person called the soil Type C and all four sides are sloped. If one side is shored or boxed, the numbers change. Source: 29 CFR 1926 Subpart P, Appendix B. The project spec governs.
